I spent five years at Berkeley on both sides of the classroom — degrees in computer science and economics, then a master's, while working my way from uGSI up to Lead TA, Head TA, and finally Lecturer for CS/Data 100. Teaching a course of 1,200 students shows you exactly where it strains: institutional knowledge evaporates between semesters, and the students who most need help are often the least able to ask for it. My thesis, MilOH, is one answer — a retrieval-augmented tool that gives TAs enough context to spend office hours teaching instead of decoding. I've also tested hypotheses on competition networks, surveyed what makes a model interpretable, and built a pill dispenser that won $25k in Portugal. The thread never changes: every learner deserves the tools to ask and answer their most ambitious questions.
